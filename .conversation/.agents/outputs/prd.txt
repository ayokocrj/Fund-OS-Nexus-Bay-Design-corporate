Fund OS: product requirements for the technical overhaul
of an early stage fund's tooling
Prepared for the PROfounders Capital conversation (Business Analyst & AI Automation). Author: Roland-Caryl
Obam. Version 0.1, 4 October 2026. Status: draft for the discovery call. Audience: partners, the analyst who will own
the system, and whoever signs off on data access.
Conventions. Requirements are tagged MUST, SHOULD or COULD. Every number is one of three kinds and is
labelled as such: public (with its source), fund-provided (to be measured in week one of the pilot), or illustrative (my
assumption, to be replaced by the fund's own figure). No price appears in this document; commercial terms are
discussed on the call.
0. Why this document exists
The role description asks for one thing in two halves: structure and track portfolio and financial data, and build the AI
and automation system that runs the full pipeline from sourcing to screening to diligence to portfolio monitoring, with
investor reporting and audits underneath. This PRD treats that as what it is: a technical overhaul of a fund's tooling,
delivered as six modules on one platform, each with a measurable financial upside, a scoping method, a build plan, a
production standard and an improvement loop.
It also exists to draw a line against a category of tool that is easy to build and dangerous to run. The reference demo
shared alongside this document (an "AI VC agent" that reads a deck, chats about valuation, drafts a memo,
interprets a financial model and emails a $200k term sheet to a founder, end to end, with no human step) shows
every capability a fund would want and none of the properties a fund can live with: no ground truth, no evaluation, no
audit trail, no integration with the system of record, an external action taken by a machine, and numbers it cannot
explain. Fund OS is built from the opposite end: ground truth first, evaluation underneath, every external action held
for a person.
1. Context and problem statement
1.1 What the fund is, in its own words
Pan-European early stage fund, backing "the fixers": founders repairing broken customer experiences. Tickets of
€500k to €3m, lead or syndicate. Companies with a working product and early validation, European HQ or significant
presence, tech-enabled B2B or B2C. Twenty-six active portfolio companies in about ten countries, thirty-two exits,
investing since 2009; the fund meets thousands of companies a year and invests in six to eight (source:
profounderscapital.com, read 28 September 2026).
1.2 What the numbers say about where the work is
A survey of 885 VCs reports roughly a hundred opportunities considered per deal closed, with about one in four
reaching a management meeting, one third of those a partner meeting, half of those due diligence, and 118 hours of
due diligence on average per closed deal over about 83 days (Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan, Strebulaev, "How Do
Venture Capitalists Make Decisions?", Journal of Financial Economics, 2020, NBER w22587; fractions rounded).
Three quarters of the work of a fund is deciding what not to meet. Each of those decisions already carries a label in
the fund's CRM.
1.3 The problems, as I expect to find them (to be confirmed in discovery)
Deal flow lives in inboxes and a CRM that stores decisions but does not learn from them. First passes are
inconsistent across people, days and fatigue.

1.4 What "good" looks like in twelve months
One pipeline from inbox to decision with a consistent, explained first pass; one monthly portfolio schema with flags
the day an update lands; benchmarks and the LP letter drafted from the same table; diligence memos that start at
eighty percent; a document vault that makes an audit a query, not a hunt. And, under all of it, a weekly read-out that
says how well the system agrees with partners, so that trust is earned by numbers.
2. Goals, non-goals, success metrics
2.1 Goals
1. Reduce the cost and raise the consistency of the first pass on inbound deals, measured against the fund's own
past decisions.
2. Turn portfolio monitoring from collection work into exception handling.
3. Produce LP-facing material (quarterly letter, benchmarks, data requests) from a single source of truth.
4. Make every automated decision auditable: logged, bounded, measured.
5. Leave the fund owning the system: code, data, keys, documentation.
2.2 Non-goals (for the first year)
Replacing partner judgement on investment decisions. The system assists diligence and never decides.
Automating any outbound communication to founders or LPs without a person sending it.
Sourcing automation before screening is measured. Sourcing has no ground truth inside the fund.
Valuation, term sheet generation or legal drafting. The reference demo does this; a fund should not.
A new CRM. The system sits on top of the existing one.

4. Principles (non-negotiable design rules)
1. Automate where a decision already has a label; assist where it does not; never let the machine act outside the
fund on its own.
2. Ground truth before models. No module ships before its gold set exists and its bar is signed.
3. Every automated output carries its inputs, its reasons and its cost, in one log record, queryable by a partner in
one click.
4. Human-in-the-loop invariants are code, not policy: no message to a founder or an LP, no archive, no rejection, no
criteria change without a named person.
5. Built inside the fund's stack (Google Workspace, the existing CRM, the existing data room), with accounts and
API keys in the fund's name, revocable any day.
6. Models are replaceable components behind an interface; any change is an A/B on the gold set before it ships.
7. Data stays in the fund's tenant. Nothing is trained on the fund's data outside the fund's control.
8. Boring cadence: kickoff, Friday Loom, biweekly check-in, the fund's own test week, a definition of done written
before the build.

5.1 Data model (minimum)
Deal: source, received_at, company_id, stage, status, owner, decision (meet, pass, keep warm), decision_by,
decision_at, score, score_reasons, gaps, criteria_version.
Company: legal name, HQ country, website, registry id, model (B2B, B2C), sector tags, founders, round sought,
ticket fit.
Decision: deal_id, person, outcome, timestamp, reason free text. This table is the training and test set for M1.
Metric: company_id, period, kpi (growth, net burn, runway, gross margin, NRR, CAC payback, burn multiple),
value, source, confidence, flag.
Document: owner entity, type, version, hash, location, retention class, provenance.
Run: module, inputs (hashes), model and prompt version, outputs, reason, cost, latency, status, invariant checks,
reviewer.
5.2 Integration inventory (to confirm in discovery)
Mailbox (shared deal flow inbox), CRM (Affinity, HubSpot, Notion or Airtable, whichever is in place), Google
Workspace (Drive, Sheets, Forms), data room (DocSend, Drive, Dropbox), cap table and fund admin (Carta or
equivalent, read only), company registries (Companies House, INPI, Brønnøysund and peers via public APIs),
calendar.
6. Module requirements
Each module lists purpose, requirements, invariants, evaluation, data, financial upside with its formula, and rollout.
Effort is given as a range in person-weeks for one builder; it is an estimate, not a commitment.
M1. Screening: from inbox to a partner decision
Purpose. Turn every inbound opportunity into one CRM record with a score against the fund's written thesis, the
evidence found, the gaps, and a position in a ranked queue; the partner decides.
Requirements. - M1-F1 MUST ingest from the shared inbox, web form and CRM "new" status; dedupe by domain
and founder email. - M1-F2 MUST extract a fixed schema from deck, website and registry (company, HQ, model,
product status, traction, round, prior investors), each field with a source pointer and a confidence. - M1-F3 MUST
score against versioned criteria derived from the published thesis (broken customer experience, working product,
early validation, European HQ or significant presence, tech-enabled B2B or B2C, stage and ticket fit) with weights set
with a partner in week one; the score MUST carry the reasons per criterion and the list of gaps. - M1-F4 MUST write
one record per deal to the CRM with status "scored"; MUST NOT change any status beyond that. - M1-F5 MUST

Evaluation. Gold set: the last 200 inbound decisions with outcomes, extended monthly. Metrics: recall on "took a
meeting", precision on "meet", minutes per deal including review, disagreement rate, drift versus previous week.
Weekly read-out on Friday; alarm when recall drops more than 5 points.
Financial upside. Formula: (inbound per year × minutes manual first pass) minus (inbound per year × (machine
minute + human review minutes)), valued at the analyst's loaded hourly cost; plus the unmeasured value of the deal
nobody had time to open. Illustrative: 1,500 inbound × 20 min = 500 h; after: 1,500 × 4 min of review = 100 h; 400 h a
year, about ten working weeks, at the fund's own hourly cost. Consistency is the second upside: the same thesis
applied on a Friday night and a Monday morning. Both replaced by the fund's numbers in week one.
Effort. 4 to 6 person-weeks for the pilot, 2 to 3 more for production hardening.
M2. Portfolio monitoring: one form, one schema, flags the day the update lands
Purpose. Collect the same six KPIs from every portfolio company every month, normalise them, flag anomalies, and
give partners one page.
Requirements. - M2-F1 MUST provide a founder update form (web form or email template parsed on receipt) with the
KPI schema: ARR or MRR growth, net burn and runway, gross margin, NRR or cohort retention, CAC payback, burn
multiple; each with period, currency, source and a free-text comment. - M2-F2 MUST parse board decks and update
emails into the same schema when a founder does not use the form, with confidence and a source pointer per value;
low-confidence values are queued for the analyst, not written as facts. - M2-F3 MUST compute derived metrics
(runway from cash and net burn, burn multiple from net burn and net new ARR) and show the formula. - M2-F4
MUST raise flags against thresholds set per company and stage (illustrative: runway under 12 months; growth below
plan two months running; gross margin falling two quarters; NRR under 100 %; CAC payback over 18 months; burn
multiple over 2.0x) the same day the update lands. - M2-F5 MUST chase missing updates with a draft reminder
saved for the analyst to send; never sent automatically. - M2-F6 SHOULD show per-company history and the
portfolio page with percentiles (see M3). - M2-F7 COULD ingest bank statements or management accounts shared
by the company for reconciliation.
Invariants. No message to a founder; no value written below the confidence bar without review; every value traceable
to a source.
Evaluation. Completeness by day 10 of the month; parse accuracy on a labelled set of 50 past updates; time from
update to flag; false flag rate reviewed monthly with a partner.
Financial upside. Formula: companies × monthly collection and normalisation time × 12, at the analyst's loaded
hourly cost, plus the option value of early warning (one runway problem seen three months earlier is a bridge
negotiated instead of a write-off; unmeasured, to be tracked as cases). Illustrative: 26 companies × 45 minutes × 12
= about 230 hours a year.
Effort. 3 to 5 person-weeks.

summary, company highlights, flags resolved) from the metric table, with every figure linked to its source row;

M4. Diligence memo assist: the memo starts at eighty percent
Purpose. Ingest a data room and produce the factual sections of the investment memo with citations, a red-flag list
and a question list, for the deal team to complete.
Requirements. - M4-F1 MUST ingest a data room (folder of PDFs, spreadsheets, decks) and build an index with
document provenance. - M4-F2 MUST draft the factual sections of the fund's own memo template (company,
product, market as described by the company, traction, team, round, cap table summary) with a citation per figure to
the source document and page. - M4-F3 MUST produce a red-flag list (inconsistencies between documents, missing
standard documents, metrics that do not reconcile) and a question list for management. - M4-F4 MUST mark
anything not found in the data room as "not in data room" rather than filling from general knowledge. - M4-F5
SHOULD extract the financial model into the fund's standard sheet with a reconciliation check (totals, cash, burn) and
highlight interpretation choices for a human to accept or reject. - M4-F6 COULD run a reference-check tracker (who,
when, notes) owned by the deal lead.
Invariants. No investment recommendation; no valuation; no outbound request to the company or references; no
figure without a citation.
Evaluation. Share of sections accepted without rewrite; citation accuracy on a sample checked by the deal lead; red
flags later confirmed versus missed, reviewed per deal.
Financial upside. Formula: diligence hours per closed deal (public average 118 h, Gompers et al.) × share spent on
extraction and first drafting (fund-provided) × deals per year. Illustrative: 20 % of 118 h on 7 deals is about 165 h a
year, and faster memos mean faster answers to founders, which is a sourcing advantage.
Effort. 4 to 6 person-weeks.
M5. Sourcing signals: only after screening is measured
Purpose. Surface companies that match the thesis before they reach the inbox, from public signals, scored with the
same criteria as M1, for a partner to decide whether to reach out.
Requirements. - M5-F1 MUST collect public signals for the fund's geographies (new company registrations in target
categories, job postings indicating product traction, app store releases, open source activity for developer-facing
products, press and accelerator cohorts) with the source stored. - M5-F2 MUST score candidates with M1's criteria
and show the evidence; low-evidence candidates are not shown. - M5-F3 MUST hold any outreach as a draft in the
partner's mailbox with a reason line; never sent automatically. - M5-F4 SHOULD learn from the partner's "worth a
look" and "no" marks as a second gold set. - M5-F5 COULD track warm paths (shared investors, portfolio founders)
to a candidate.
Invariants. No outreach; no scraping outside sources permitted by their terms; signals stored with their licence.

M6. Fund operations and audits: an audit becomes a query
Purpose. A document vault and a decision register that answer auditor, administrator and LP requests in minutes,
with retention and provenance.
Requirements. - M6-F1 MUST maintain a vault of fund documents (LPA and side letters as provided, investment
documents, board materials, valuations from the administrator, KPI tables, LP letters) with version, hash, owner and
retention class. - M6-F2 MUST keep the decision register (every IC decision, every M1 override, every criteria
change) with who, when and why. - M6-F3 MUST answer standard requests ("all documents for company X in 2026",
"KPI history for the portfolio Q1 to Q3") as exports with a provenance sheet. - M6-F4 SHOULD run compliance
checks the fund defines (for example: board materials received per company per quarter; KYC documents present
per LP) and raise flags to the ops owner. - M6-F5 COULD prepare the annual audit pack as a checklist with links.
Invariants. No document deleted by the system; no document shared outside the tenant by the system; access by
role; every access logged.
Evaluation. Time to answer standard requests; completeness checks; audit findings related to documentation
trending to zero.
Financial upside. Formula: days per year on audit and LP request preparation (fund-provided) × loaded cost, plus
lower audit fees if the auditor's hours fall (to confirm with the auditor), plus reduced key-person risk when knowledge
is in a system rather than in one inbox.
Effort. 3 to 5 person-weeks, phased with M3.
7. The evaluation and reliability layer (cross-cutting)
7.1 Gold sets
One per module, owned by the fund: M1 the last 200 inbound decisions; M2 fifty labelled past updates; M3 the last
four letters with their source data; M4 two past data rooms with their final memos; M5 partner marks collected
monthly; M6 last year's audit requests and answers. Extended monthly; stored in the fund's tenant.
7.2 Metrics and the Friday read-out
Every Friday the system produces one page per live module: bar, this week, last week, disagreements to review,
incidents, cost. Precision and recall for M1 and M5; completeness and parse accuracy for M2; reconciliation and
corrections for M3; acceptance and citation accuracy for M4; request latency for M6. A partner reviews
disagreements in a twenty-minute slot; every override becomes a training example.
7.3 Invariants, enforced in code
No message to a founder or an LP; no deal rejected or archived without a person; no data leaving the tenant; no
score or figure without a logged reason or source; no criteria or threshold change without a partner's sign-off; no
model change without an A/B on the gold set.

7.4 Failure handling
Retries, then a fallback model, then a stop; never a silent skip. Minor failures routed to the builder, major ones to a
partner within the hour. Test and production separated; nothing ships untested. Every run has one log record: inputs,
model and prompt version, output, reason, cost, latency, invariant results, reviewer.
7.5 Drift and improvement loop
Weekly: metrics against bar; monthly: gold set extended, thresholds reviewed; quarterly: criteria version review with
partners, model A/B if a candidate exists, roadmap of the next module. Any change is logged in the decision register
(M6-F2).
8. Data, security and compliance
Tenant: all data, logs and gold sets live in the fund's Google Workspace, CRM and chosen database; the builder's
access is through accounts created in the fund's name and revocable any day.
Secrets: API keys in the fund's secret manager; never in code or documents; usage billed to the fund's accounts.
Personal data: founders' and LPs' personal data processed under UK GDPR and GDPR with a documented
purpose; retention classes per document type; a deletion procedure for passed deals after the period the fund
sets.
Regulated status: the fund is FCA authorised (reference on its website). The system takes no decision and sends
no communication; it produces records. Records retention and access logging support, rather than complicate,
the fund's obligations; confirm specifics with the fund's compliance officer in scoping.
Model providers: approved list set by the fund; data processing terms reviewed; no training on fund data; option
to route sensitive modules (M4, M6) to models hosted in the fund's cloud tenant.
Access: role-based (partner, analyst, ops, builder, read-only auditor); every access logged.
9. Scoping strategy (phase 0, two weeks)
Objective: leave scoping with a signed definition of done for M1, a baseline, data access, and a gold set.
1. Thirty-minute call with a partner: how a deal moves from inbox to partner meeting today; where the first pass
happens; who decides; what the CRM records.
2. Data access: export of the last 200 inbound deals with outcomes; read access to the shared inbox and CRM;
sample of founder updates and the last two LP letters.
3. Baseline measured, not estimated: minutes per deal on ten real first passes timed with the analyst; completeness
of last month's portfolio KPIs; days spent on the last letter.
4. Criteria workshop (one hour, partner plus analyst): the published thesis turned into six criteria with evidence
definitions and weights; thresholds for recall, precision and minutes per deal; the stop rule.
5. Definition of done written as verifiable bullets; out of scope listed; the fund's obligations (access, response time)
listed.
6. Deployment model chosen: A, inside the fund's stack with the builder administering (recommended); B, fully
managed by the builder, only acceptable for a demo; C, handover after any module.
7. Risk register started: data quality in the CRM, mailbox permissions, criteria disagreement between partners,
provider terms.
Discovery questions I will ask: Does deal flow live in a CRM today, or in the inbox? Who makes the first pass and
how long does it take? How many inbound opportunities a year? How are founder updates collected and where do
they go? How is the LP letter assembled and by whom? When was the last audit and what did it cost in days? Which
tools are already paid for? Who owns API keys today?

Appendix C. Glossary
Gold set: labelled examples owned by the fund used to measure the system. Shadow mode: the system scores live
deals while people decide exactly as before. Invariant: a rule checked in code before any action. Read-out: the
weekly one-page report of metrics against bar. Definition of done: the verifiable list that closes a module. Burn
multiple: net burn divided by net new ARR. NRR: net revenue retention.
